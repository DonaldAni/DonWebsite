The Tragedy of Team Fortress

10/10/26

Kayla

It's rare to be able to call a game truly perfect. You can almost always pinpoint a fundamental flaw in a game's core design that, no matter how small, hinders the playing experience.

Team Fortress 2 is no different.


Gotcha! You probably thought this was gonna be a blog post singing this game off and sucking its praises. Well kinda, but there's also actually quite a lot wrong with Team Fortress 2: Sniper is horribly designed, modern Spy is kind of really awful, there's a lot of really questionable monetization, the birth of the lootbox, etc etc. However, this is not a blog post about that. While not perfect, I truly believe that Team Fortress 2 is some of the most fun you can have online and is, at its core, a DAMN well designed game. Unfortunately, that only makes the treatment it gets from VALVe and its current state as a whole all the more saddening.

TF2 in its current state is a shambling husk of the excellent game it once was and a far cry from the timeless work of art that it had the potential to become. Almost a decade of neglect and bare-minimum updates have left this game a rotted, bloating corpse full of maps that entered the rotation once, hundreds of hats that have only been inside crates for under 6 months, and dozens of duplicate unusual effects. This game is almost 30 damn gigabytes and it continues to grow despite the fact that nothing of note has been added to the game for, at the time of writing, 3280 days.

This is absolutely unacceptable.

I know I said I didn't plan to glaze, but Team Fortress 2 is one of the best designed video games that I've ever played. The intricate dance between two players in a one-on-one and the game of tug-of-war that happens in the frontlines of team fights is something that you just don't see anymore in shooters, coming from someone who isn't the biggest fan of shooter games. Every class has a role (no matter how stupid) and for the most part the game wouldn't be nearly as enjoyable if one of them were not there. The damage numbers are so finely tuned and the mechanics of the game are carefully crafted to encourage the type of gameplay that TF2 really shines at: getting in each other's faces and working with your team to gain space.

Even beyond gameplay, TF2's art style and environment designs (at least the maps made by VALVe that were there at release) come together to make a game that immerses you without having a shred of realism to it. This, too, has degraded over time, with updates that push the game further away from its cartoony art style and closer to generic Source game funk by removing glossy PBR materials in favor of more 'accurate' lighting, which is not helped by the newer maps, mostly contributed by the community rather than made in-house, that tend to do much more "out there" concepts. There's much to say about this topic, but I'm not the most qualified to talk about a game's art style. I've linked a good YouTube video about the game's visual degradation over time below.

---

I'm not the type of person to believe things should last forever. I think that Team Fortress 2 has lived its natural lifespan for a game and after decades of content updates is pretty solid. With community servers, this game honestly will never die. What I'm not okay with is VALVe's insistence on pumping out low-effort workshop seasonal updates that do very little for the game other than add hats to buy. I believe that the best fate for this game would be VALVe taking their hands off completely to let this game live out its sunset years in a state of relative quality, rather than continue to sink deeper into live-service hell.
